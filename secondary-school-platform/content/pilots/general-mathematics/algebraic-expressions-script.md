# Pilot audio lesson script — Algebraic expressions

**Asset status:** Draft pilot script only  
**Subject:** General Mathematics  
**Class / term:** Not assigned — curriculum mapping pending  
**NERDC alignment:** Not claimed; requires source capture and teacher review  
**Format:** Two-presenter educational conversation  
**Target duration:** 5–7 minutes  
**Version:** 0.1

## Learning goals
By the end, a learner should be able to:
1. Identify variables, coefficients and constants in an expression.
2. Collect like terms.
3. Expand a simple bracket.
4. Substitute a value into an expression.

## Script

**[Intro music — original or licensed only; 3 seconds]**

**Presenter A:** Welcome to Learnwise. Today we're making algebraic expressions easier to understand. You will hear a few examples, so keep a notebook nearby.

**Presenter B:** Let's begin with a simple question. In the expression three x plus five, what does the letter x mean?

**Presenter A:** The letter x is a variable. It represents a number whose value may change. The number multiplying it is called the coefficient. So, in three x, the coefficient is three. The number five is a constant because it has no variable attached.

**Presenter B:** So if x is four, three x plus five becomes three times four plus five?

**Presenter A:** Exactly. Three times four is twelve, and twelve plus five is seventeen.

**[Pause — 2 seconds]**

**Presenter B:** Now, what are like terms?

**Presenter A:** Like terms have the same variable part. For example, three x and two x are like terms. We can add their coefficients: three plus two gives five. Therefore, three x plus two x simplifies to five x.

**Presenter B:** What about three x plus two y? Can we combine those?

**Presenter A:** No. The variable parts are different. We keep them as three x plus two y.

**Presenter B:** Let's try a longer expression: four a plus three minus a plus two.

**Presenter A:** First group the like terms. Four a minus a gives three a. Three plus two gives five. The simplified expression is three a plus five.

**Presenter B:** Let's move to brackets. How do we expand two times the bracket a plus three?

**Presenter A:** Multiply the two by every term inside the bracket. Two times a is two a. Two times three is six. The result is two a plus six.

**Presenter B:** So we distribute the outside factor to each term inside.

**Presenter A:** Correct. Remember: every term inside the bracket must be multiplied.

**[Worked-example pause]**

**Presenter B:** Your turn. Simplify six x minus two x plus seven.

**[Pause — 5 seconds]**

**Presenter A:** Combine six x and minus two x. Six minus two is four, so the answer is four x plus seven.

**Presenter B:** One more. Expand three times the bracket two y minus four.

**[Pause — 5 seconds]**

**Presenter A:** Three times two y is six y. Three times minus four is minus twelve. The answer is six y minus twelve.

**Presenter B:** Let's recap. A variable represents a number. A coefficient multiplies a variable. Like terms have the same variable part. To expand a bracket, multiply the outside factor by every term inside.

**Presenter A:** Before you go, try simplifying five m plus two minus three m plus six, and expand four times the bracket n plus two. Check your work by grouping like terms and distributing carefully.

**Presenter B:** Keep practising, and we'll meet you in the next lesson.

**[Outro — 2 seconds]**

## Answer key for editorial review
1. Five m plus two minus three m plus six = two m plus eight.
2. Four times the bracket n plus two = four n plus eight.

## Production notes
- Pronounce algebraic symbols consistently: “x”, “y”, “coefficient”, “like terms”, “expand”.
- Do not read “2(a + 3)” as “two a plus three”; say “two multiplied by the quantity a plus three”.
- Add silence only at the marked practice pauses.
- Record the final transcript from the approved audio master, not from an earlier script version.
- Obtain subject-teacher review, rights confirmation and audio QA before publication.
